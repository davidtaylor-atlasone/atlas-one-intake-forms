Benchmark sources & assumptions. Support/contact-center turnover runs 41 to 46% annually (higher in first year); replacing one agent costs roughly 50 to 200% of pay (Gallup) or ~$10k to $46k all-in, with 5 to 8 weeks to hire and up to 6 to 8 months to full ramp. Retaining a customer typically costs 5× less than acquiring one (range 3 to 25× by industry; original "5×" from Bain/HBR, 1990); a 5% lift in retention can raise profits 25 to 95%. Existing customers convert at 60 to 70% vs 5 to 20% for new prospects. One rep supports roughly 250 to 500 customers in SaaS/B2B (HubSpot ~1:350) and 400 to 600 in e-commerce, though high-touch/account-based support can be 10 to 50; that ratio is what makes the agent cost comparable per customer. CAC varies enormously by industry: the $200 default is illustrative only; use your own. On customer experience: up to 32% of customers walk away from a brand they love after a single bad experience (PwC); ~40% of departures trace to how they felt about the service; poor service costs U.S. businesses tens of billions a year (NewVoiceMedia est. $75B). Lifetime value here is measured in gross margin, not revenue, so the ROI figures are real profit. Figures are directional planning benchmarks, not a substitute for your actual books.
